# FAQ & Help — Reliable Car Under $12,000 (Houston)

Data stamp: Listings from Facebook Marketplace sweep, Oct 26, 2026 — listings sell fast; a car shown here may already be gone.

Owner's criteria: under $12,000 cash, sedan, ≤75,000 miles, factory Android Auto + reverse camera required. Hybrid and lane assist preferred. Reliability first.

**Honest headline:** Only 1 of the 32 cars in the Oct 26 sweep meets ALL must-haves — the 2019 Kia Forte FE in Spring, TX ($7,000, 44,500 mi). It is a manual transmission car.

Jargon in one line: **Trim** = version of the same car (LX basic, EX mid, Touring high). **CVT** = automatic with a belt instead of fixed gears. Hybrid **eCVT** is a different, simpler, stronger device — not a belt CVT. **PPI** = pre-purchase inspection by your own mechanic. **Android Auto (AA)** = Google's system that puts your phone's maps/calls/music on the car's screen; in this budget it is wired (USB cable) on effectively every car.

More jargon: **KSDS** = Hyundai/Kia knock-sensor software that detects engine knock and limps the car — a warning system, not a repair. **UVO** = Kia's infotainment system. **MyLink** = Chevrolet's infotainment system. **Display Audio** = Honda's touchscreen system.

## Which cars should I pursue?

- **2019 Ford Fusion Hybrid SE** — first choice. Proven hybrid system, AA standard, safety kit standard. Regular Hybrid only — never the Energi plug-in.
- **2017–18 Fusion Hybrid SE / Fusion 2.5L SE with the 8-inch SYNC 3 screen** — same idea, older. Small 4.2-inch screen = no AA, ever.
- **2016–17 Honda Accord EX/EX-L, 2.4L** and **Honda Civic Sedan 2.0L — 2016–18 EX and above; 2019 Sport (the only 2019 2.0L with Android Auto)** — Avoid 2019 EX and above — that's the 1.5T. Test the Civic's A/C hard; Honda extended the condenser warranty on some 2016–17 Civics.
- **2019 Kia Forte (any trim)** and **2018 Hyundai Elantra SEL** — value picks. A 2019 Elantra needs proof the piston-ring recall (21V-301) was done.
- **2018–19 Subaru Legacy Premium, late-2018/2019 Mazda6 Touring (non-turbo), 2017–19 Chevy Impala LT V6, 2020 Toyota Camry** — alternatives, each with one named check in RESEARCH.md.

## Which should I avoid?

- **Ford Fusion 1.5L EcoBoost** — coolant can leak into the cylinders; engine replacement can cost $3,500–$8,000.
- **Honda 1.5T (Accord/Civic 2016–20)** — oil dilution: fuel thins the oil, especially on short trips.
- **Hyundai Sonata / Kia Optima 2.4L & 2.0T "Theta II"** — connecting-rod bearing failure, knock, seizure, fire risk. Any knock or patchy oil history: walk.
- **Nissan Altima 2013–18, Ford Focus/Fiesta automatics** — CVT / PowerShift dual-clutch failures. The Fusion never had PowerShift — do not confuse them.
- **Plug-in hybrids in this budget (Fusion Energi, Sonata Plug-in)** — aged big batteries and a Fusion Energi fire recall (do-not-charge).
- **Any rebuilt / salvage / flood title, any seller who refuses an inspection** — in Houston, that rule alone saves most bad buys.

## Check a VIN — in this order

1. **NHTSA VIN Decoder (free)** — https://www.nhtsa.gov/vin-decoder — proves the VIN matches the year/make/model/engine in the ad. Proves nothing about condition or history.
2. **NHTSA Recalls (free)** — https://www.nhtsa.gov/recalls — proves which safety recalls are still open on that exact car. Does not show completed recalls — get a dealer printout for those.
3. **NICB VINCheck (free, 5/day)** — https://www.nicb.org/vincheck — proves an insurer reported it stolen or totalled. A clean result does NOT guarantee a clean car.
4. **NMVTIS via TxDMV Title Check (~$10–20)** — https://vehiclehistory.gov / https://www.txdmv.gov (search "Title Check") — title brands from any state (Salvage, Rebuilt, Flood, odometer brands). This is the title-washing detector.
5. **Carfax (~$45–50) or AutoCheck (~$25–30)** — https://www.carfax.com / https://www.autocheck.com — paid history for a finalist only. Carfax is stronger on service detail; AutoCheck on auctions. Neither proves anything never reported — believe a bad inspection over a clean report.
6. **In person (free, non-negotiable)** — VIN plate (windshield) = door sticker = title, and the seller's photo ID name = name on the title. Any mismatch: stop.

## Pre-purchase inspection checklist

$100–$150, your mechanic (not the seller's), seller who refuses = walk.

- Full scan of all modules; hybrid battery cell voltages on hybrids; watch for cleared codes.
- Cold start observed — never a pre-warmed car. Listen for knock/rattle; watch smoke.
- Oil & coolant: over-full oil smelling of fuel (dilution), glitter/metal in oil (bearing wear), coolant pressure test.
- On a lift: crash/flood signs, frame rails, paint thickness, suspension, leaks, tyre dates — all four tyres matching on AWD.
- Houston flood protocol, every car: silt under carpets / spare-tyre well, rusty seat rails and under-dash metal, musty smell, fogged lamps.
- A/C torture test: max cold 10 minutes, vent temperature measured — critical on Civics.
- Road test: CVT judder, low-speed shudder, highway merge, straight braking, whine with radio off.
- Your phone + cable: Android Auto launch, reverse camera, every screen function (Mazda "ghost touch" watch, 60 seconds).
- Written list of what is due now — add it to the price before negotiating.
- Walk away for: frame repair, flood evidence, metal in fluids, bad hybrid cell spread, title/odometer mismatch.

## Marketplace scam red flags

- **Curbstoner:** "private seller" with many cars sold, parking-lot meets, cannot say how long they owned it.
- **Title jumping:** title in a stranger's name, blank buyer line. ID must match title — no match, no money.
- **Washed salvage:** 25–40% under market, fresh out-of-state title, "clean blue title!" — run NICB + NMVTIS first. Note: in Texas a clean title is itself blue — what matters is the brand printed on the title (e.g. "Rebuilt Salvage"), not the colour, so verify the brand on the physical title.
- **Bait listing:** stock photos, no Texas plate, pushed off-platform, deployment/divorce/shipping story.
- **Deposit scam:** never send Zelle/Cash App/Venmo to hold a car you have not stood next to.
- **Odometer games:** worn pedals/seats at "low" miles, readings going backwards in the history report.
- **"Just needs a sensor / a recharge":** price it as the full repair until inspection proves otherwise.
- Meet in daylight, public/camera-covered place, bring a second person, finish money + title at a bank or county tax office.
