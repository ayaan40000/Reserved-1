## Hybrid battery expectations at 60,000–75,000 miles

Batteries usually fade, not die suddenly: mpg drops, engine runs more, then a cell-imbalance code (e.g. P0A80). Age matters as much as miles — a 2017 pack is 9 years old in 2026.

| Hybrid | Expectation at 60–75K in 2026 | Replacement money |
|---|---|---|
| Fusion Hybrid 2017–19 (lithium-ion) | Usually serviceable; quoted life 8–10 yr / 100–150K. Budget as mid-life. Federal hybrid warranty 8yr/100K — a 2017 is out in 2026; a late 2019 may have months left (confirm in-service date). | ~$2,000–$4,000 pack + $500–$1,500 labour independent/reman; $4,000–$6,000 dealer all-in. Reconditioning $500–$1,500 is a stopgap. |
| Accord Hybrid 2017 (lithium-ion) | Similar mid-life expectation; system durable, pack is the wear item. Test the 12V too — a weak 12V mimics hybrid faults. | ~$2,000–$4,000 class — get a Houston hybrid-shop quote before buying. |
| Sonata Hybrid 2017–19 (lithium-ion polymer) | Original-owner lifetime-battery marketing does not transfer — assume no coverage, verify by VIN. Sudden hybrid-system failures exist in complaint data. | Dealer pricing can exceed Fusion/Honda — UNVERIFIED exact 2026 Houston price; a written quote is a buying condition. |
| Camry Hybrid 2020 | Youngest, healthiest pack in the guide; benchmark record. Constraint is purchase price, not the battery. | Highest cost if ever needed, lowest probability in the buying window. |

Universal hybrid PPI: scan cell/block voltage spread (big spread = fading pack); 15+ minute drive with a highway merge watching assist and mpg; ask that shop's pack price and write it into the total budget.

## Houston flood & salvage notes

Flooded cars are totalled, cleaned, and resold — sometimes after title washing (re-titling in a weaker-branding state so the brand drops off, then back to Texas). Paper can lie; national VIN systems (NMVTIS/NICB) remember the brand. Water corrodes electronics for months: intermittent electrics, airbag/module faults, returning musty smell, rust where Texas cars do not rust (seat rails, under-dash, trunk wells, connector pins).

Viewing tells (any one = flood-protocol PPI or walk): silt/mud under carpets, in the spare-tyre well, or on underbody ledges; waterline or mismatched carpet; heavy freshener + fresh detail on a cheap car; rusty seat rails/pedal brackets; fogged lamp lenses; spliced wiring; Gulf registration + sudden out-of-state title after a storm year.

Salvage economics: rebuilt cars are hard to insure for full coverage, hard for the next buyer to finance (crushing resale), and worth 20–40% less. At this budget the correct price for a rebuilt car is "no."
